# Sources: Learning French from Hungarian and/or English

Free and low-cost resources for two absolute beginners learning spoken French together, swept across structured courses, Hungarian-medium material, grammar and pronunciation references, listening input, learning method research, and couple-practice advice.

## Sources

### primary

- [Language Transfer — Complete French](https://www.languagetransfer.org/french) — **primary** · free audio course built on thinking-out-loud sentence construction; works well for two people listening together
- [TV5MONDE — Apprendre le français](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/) — **primary** · free, A1–B2 video exercises from a public francophone broadcaster; tracks your progress
- [Le français facile avec RFI](https://francaisfacile.rfi.fr/) — **primary** · daily "Journal en français facile" with transcripts and exercises; graded news listening
- [Institut français de Budapest — Nyelvtanfolyamok](https://www.franciaintezet.hu/hu/nyelvtanfolyamok) — **primary** · official A1.1 courses in Budapest, including two-person (Zoom) lessons; DELF exam centre

### reference

- [CEFR self-assessment grid (Council of Europe)](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-2-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-self-assessment-grid) — **reference** · the authoritative A1…C2 "can-do" descriptors to use as a curriculum spine
- [Lingea — Francia–magyar nyelvtan](https://szotar.lingea.hu/francia-magyar-nyelvtan) — **reference** · compact French grammar explained in Hungarian, from pronunciation to verbs
- [SZTAKI Szótár — francia–magyar](https://szotar.sztaki.hu/francia-magyar-szotar) — **reference** · the standard free Hungarian–French dictionary
- [Lawless French](https://www.lawlessfrench.com/) — **reference** · thousands of free English-language grammar and vocabulary lessons by a long-standing author
- [Kwiziq French](https://french.kwiziq.com/) — **reference** · grammar tests mapped to CEFR levels; builds a personal study plan from your mistakes
- [Wiktionary — French frequency lists](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Frequency_lists/French) — **reference** · corpus-based top-5000 word list; basis for choosing vocabulary to learn first
- [Forvo — French](https://forvo.com/languages/fr/) — **reference** · native-speaker recordings of individual words, for checking pronunciation
- [Collins — IPA pronunciation guide: French](https://blog.collinsdictionary.com/ipa-pronunciation-guide-french/) — **reference** · maps French sounds to IPA from a major dictionary publisher

### video

- [Easy French (Easy Languages)](https://www.easy-languages.org/easy-french) — **video** · unscripted street interviews with French and English subtitles, including beginner episodes
- [Francia leckék Magyaroknak (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLkVw4eJ-r2v2_o1PhtrlcvcWDkstXtmjY) — **video** · free beginner course taught in Hungarian by a Hungarian teacher

### book

- [Assimil — Le Nouveau Français sans peine (French with Ease)](https://www.assimil.com/en/with-ease/1534-le-nouveau-francais-sans-peine-3135414907298.html) — **book** · classic daily dialogue course to about B2; paid, but one copy serves two learners

### paper

- [Krashen — The Case for Comprehensible Input](https://www.sdkrashen.com/content/articles/case_for_comprehensible_input.pdf) — **paper** · original argument for input-first acquisition, which most modern methods build on
- [Beyond comprehensible input: a neuro-ecological critique (Frontiers, 2025)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1636777/full) — **paper** · recent critique: input is necessary but not enough, and speaking needs output and interaction

### article

- [Omniglot — Learning a new language together with your loved one](https://www.omniglot.com/language/articles/languagelearningwithyourlovedone.htm) — **article** · practical rules for couples: routines, no-correction sessions, target-language time

## Search angles

- Structured beginner courses from official or public bodies (TV5MONDE, RFI, Institut français)
- Hungarian-medium material (courses, grammar, dictionaries): thinner than English-medium, and mostly lower-quality commercial sites were dropped
- Grammar references in English (Lawless, Kwiziq)
- Pronunciation (IPA, liaison, nasal vowels, native recordings)
- Vocabulary frequency data
- Graded listening input (RFI, Easy French)
- Method research: comprehensible input and its critique
- Couple-specific practice advice
- Gap: no peer-reviewed study specifically on partners learning a language together, and no Hungarian-medium book course of Assimil quality was found. A Hungarian-base course may exist in print, so it is worth checking in a bookshop.
